# Folklore ledger: YouTube pass

Every claim below was asserted by a credible practitioner without method, sample, or source. None may enter the Agora authority document as a rule. Several are useful as testable priors; that is a different status and the difference is the point of this file.

Merge with the folklore appendices from the six deep research runs before drafting anything.

## Asserted effects with no method

| Claim | Source | Why it stays D |
|---|---|---|
| Changing `sign up for free` to `trial for free` doubles conversions | Vendor split-test roundup | No sample size, duration, traffic level, or significance. One brand, one page. The direction is plausible and the magnitude is not usable. |
| Matching the landing page headline to the ad beats a page-optimized headline by 115% | Same roundup | Same gap. Message-match is well supported as a principle elsewhere; this number is not evidence for it. |
| A CTA below the fold on a long page beat above-the-fold by over 300% | Same roundup | Reported as a surprise result, which is exactly when publication bias bites hardest. Useful only as a counterexample to the above-the-fold rule, which is itself D. |
| Bigger, higher-contrast CTA lifts signups 25% | Same roundup | Visual, not copy. Outside Agora's scope regardless of grade. |
| Emotional targeting produces sustained conversion gains where element testing plateaus | Talia Wolf, several appearances | Agency self-report across unnamed clients. The underlying claim that qualitative research beats element tweaking is plausible and unevidenced here. |
| Interviewing five to seven customers for fifteen to twenty minutes is sufficient | Talia Wolf | A working heuristic presented as a threshold. No basis given for the numbers. |

## Category-creation folklore, corroborated as folklore

April Dunford calls deliberate category creation "a little bit of magical thinking", and notes that very few companies that set out while small to define and then dominate a new category survived to do it. This independently corroborates the folklore item flagged in deep research prompt 4.

Her observation is Grade C practitioner judgment. It is recorded here rather than in findings because the useful output is negative: Agora should not encode category creation as a strategy, and should not encode Dunford's skepticism as a finding either.

## Method note

Absence of evidence in this pass is not evidence of absence. YouTube talks are a poor medium for method reporting even when the speaker has data, and several of these practitioners publish more rigorous material elsewhere. A D grade here means the claim arrived unsupported in this source, not that the claim is false.
