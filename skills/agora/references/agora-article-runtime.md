# Agora article writing

Use this for articles and long-form editorial work inside the existing brief, voice, argument, draft, and revision path. The entrypoint and shared writing runtime still govern factual fidelity, user authority, register, voice, house style, and ready-copy output. This is a way to develop material, not a compulsory outline.

## Find the article in the material

Before outlining, privately separate the user's required message and constraints from first-party observations, customer examples, numbers, events, decisions, failures, lessons, opinions, external sources, and voice samples. Keep straight who said what, what was counted, and what remains unknown. A voice sample supplies style habits, not article facts, unless the user also designates those facts for this article.

Choose a reader question that the available material can actually answer. Look for the consequential difference: an observation that changed a decision, a plausible answer that fails under a stated condition, two needs that cannot both be satisfied, or a familiar process whose workings are misunderstood. Use a direct explanation when no tension is needed. Do not manufacture conflict or suspense.

For a sparse brief, make the clearest useful point the known facts allow. Distinguish a missing angle from missing material. Reason through a supplied rule, compare the consequences of stated options, or clarify a useful distinction. Do not invent a customer, first-person story, personal opinion, failure, quotation, precise event, study, statistic, or outcome. Use external facts only when supplied or when the user requests research and the sources support them. Ask for a missing fact only when the requested article cannot be completed responsibly without it; otherwise make the narrower useful article.

## Develop the thought

Choose the opening for the reader's task. A lookup explanation can answer immediately. A reported feature can begin with an observed obstacle that the body investigates. In a first-party article, begin with the supplied event or decision, or the reader question it raises. Do not stage that material with an invented claim about what teams often or typically do.

Let the strength and importance of the material determine section order and length. Privately name what the next passage adds: an explanation, consequence, test, objection, changed understanding, or decision. Follow the dependency between ideas instead of filling equal sections. A later paragraph should make use of what the reader has already learned; avoid restarting the topic with each heading.

Give a useful example enough room to do its work. Explain which detail matters and what follows from it. When the material permits, carry the same example into the next complication instead of replacing it with another undeveloped example. A number, quotation, or scene earns space through what it explains, not merely through being concrete. Introduce background when the reader needs it to understand the next move.

In analysis, take the strongest relevant alternative or objection seriously and show how it changes the recommendation. Develop supported reasoning without presenting an inference as an observation or inventing the author's beliefs. In practical accounts, retain a failed step when it explains why the next step changed. Chronology alone is not an argument, and an exhaustive diary can hide the decision.

Preserve attribution and certainty. Do not turn a supplied recommendation into an unattributed command, a customer report into a measured result, or a possibility into an event. Carry any supplied unmeasured outcome or negative limit into visible copy once when it bounds the conclusion. Place it beside the inference it limits rather than repeatedly narrating what the writer can prove.

## Finish and revise

End at the state the material reaches: an answer, a decision, an observed result, a bounded next step, or a question that remains open. A useful intervention can finish while its cause remains unresolved. Keep a supplied closing detail when it changes the reader's understanding; do not add a decorative callback, maxim, triumph, or recap to manufacture closure.

During revision, replace generic sentences with the user's concrete details and develop the connections between them. Read the middle independently of the opening: does it move the thought forward? Remove paragraphs that repeat a point without adding a relationship or consequence. Keep useful complexity and the author's credible individual choices. If it sounds like a legal brief, source review, or writing assessment, recast the passage in ordinary words.

Follow an outline, section order, or format the user explicitly requests. When headings appear in a numbered instruction list, treat the numbers as list markers unless the user asks for numbered headings. Trace the user's instructions, first-party material, required facts, length, structure, and active voice through the final article. Run the shared final checks and return only the requested deliverable.
