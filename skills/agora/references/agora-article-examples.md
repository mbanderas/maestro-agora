# Article teaching examples

## How to use these examples

Read only the section nearest the article's genre or current writing problem. Each is an original synthetic demonstration with a closed fact set, not a published excerpt, author profile, article fact source, or required structure. Transfer the editorial decision, not its wording or subject matter. The user's requested structure and voice take priority. Skip a move that the material does not support.

## Reported feature: develop a consequence

Facts: A community print room owns twelve frames. Six have handles on the left and six on the right. Its keeper marks the handle side in the catalogue. Members still choose a frame in person. Marking is incomplete; no effect on borrowing has been measured.

Thin: "The print room has twelve frames, with handles on different sides. The keeper is marking the catalogue. Members choose their frames in person. The effect has not been measured."

Developed: "The print room's catalogue is acquiring a small distinction: which side of a frame carries the handle. Six handles are on the left and six on the right. The keeper's marks can tell a member which kind of frame is listed, although members still choose in person. Marking is unfinished, and the room has not measured any change in borrowing."

Decision: Make the catalogue change intelligible before asking the reader to care about it. Keep the stated limit beside the proposed use. The example earns a modest observation; it cannot support a story about better accessibility or happier members.

## Explanation: make a distinction usable

Facts: A seed envelope names 30 seeds. A germination test started with ten seeds; eight sprouted. The test describes that sample, not the eventual plants in a buyer's garden. No growing conditions or garden result are supplied.

Thin: "Seed count and germination are different. The packet has 30 seeds and the test had an 80 percent germination rate. Results may vary."

Developed: "The two numbers answer different questions. Thirty is how many seeds the envelope contains. Eight out of ten is how many sprouted in the test sample. The second figure does not count what will grow in a buyer's garden. It describes the test, whose growing conditions are not supplied here."

Decision: Keep each number attached to what was counted. The explanation advances from quantity to tested behaviour, then to the limit of applying that result. An analogy or an invented gardening story would add little. Do not turn the ratio into a guarantee of 24 plants.

## Analysis: test the tempting answer

Facts: A club can rent a projector for EUR 60 per event or buy one for EUR 420. It has four events confirmed and three proposed. Both prices cover the required equipment. Storage and resale values are unknown. The task permits a recommendation about current commitments.

Thin: "Buying may be cost-effective in the long term. Renting offers flexibility. The club should consider its needs before deciding."

Developed: "Four confirmed events would cost EUR 240 in rentals. Buying would commit another EUR 180 before the club knows whether the other events will happen. If all three proposed events go ahead, seven rentals would equal the EUR 420 purchase price. For the events already committed, renting costs less; the proposed programme supplies a reason to revisit that choice. Storage and resale remain unpriced."

Decision: Let the strongest argument for buying enter the calculation and show the condition under which it matters. The recommendation follows the stated commitments. Do not infer equipment quality, affordability, future demand, or a general rule that renting is better.

## First person: show a decision changing

Facts: The fictional author took four photographs of a glaze test. Three lacked a label in the frame. The author could not connect those three photographs to the test notes and plans to put the label beside the next test. The author has not yet tried this change. First-person narration is authorized.

Thin: "I learned the importance of keeping good records. Next time I will improve my documentation so that my tests are more useful."

Developed: "I had four photographs and test notes. Only one photograph included a label, so I could not connect the other three to what I had written. Next time I plan to put the label beside the test before taking the picture. I have not tried that yet."

Decision: Show the observation that changed the next action. The author can own the experience without acquiring an invented emotion, quirk, or triumphant result. In a task with real voice samples, express this reasoning in that voice instead of copying this example's cadence.
