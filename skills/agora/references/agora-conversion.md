# Agora conversion context

## Purpose

Use this reference when the user asks Agora to improve conversion, design a funnel experiment, compare variants, or write for a landing page, product page, pricing page, paywall, checkout, form, upgrade, or onboarding flow where a measurable action matters.

Treat conversion patterns as contextual priors, not laws. This reference does not create a primary mode or modifier. It operates inside `SELL` or `TRANSACT` and preserves user authority. Never treat an offer, KPI, field, permission, proof item, or claim as a supplied fact when it is not. Propose one only when the user asks for recommendations or the deliverable requires it.

## Route the decision before drafting

Identify these variables from the brief:

1. The decision surface and funnel stage.
2. The action and commitment the reader is considering.
3. The business outcome the user wants to change.
4. The largest live uncertainty or perceived effort at that decision.
5. The audience's awareness, involvement, and reason for choosing.
6. The proof, terms, reversibility, and risk information available.

Do not substitute a nearby metric for the stated objective. A click is not activation, a completed form is not a qualified lead, purchase intent is not a purchase, checkout completion is not retained revenue, and revenue is not profit.

When no performance objective is supplied, optimize the requested writing for comprehension, decision fit, and the named next action. Do not invent a company KPI or claim a likely lift.

## Use patterns as bounded priors

### Clear action and destination

Keep CTA wording concrete and predictable under `CTA standard`. This is an operational-clarity rule, not a claim that one label converts better in every context.

### Closed-world conversion briefs

When the user supplies facts and says to use only those facts, every conversion claim, reassurance, mechanism, route, term, and operational detail must come from that set or follow from it directly. Do not fill ordinary page slots with plausible industry defaults.

- Payment processed by a named provider does not establish security, encryption, a particular widget, or a field layout.
- A badge described as self-made is not verified proof. Explicitly direct its removal from a recommended trust composition unless the user requires it to remain; omission from the placement list is not enough. Do not relabel it as fake, fabricated, deceptive, or untrustworthy.
- Product fit does not establish adapter, trimming, installation, airflow, or performance behavior that was not supplied.
- A trial does not establish automatic billing, renewal, access after expiration, or cancellation mechanics.
- A cancellation right does not establish where cancellation occurs, when access ends, or whether a refund or cooling-off period exists.
- A monthly renewal term does not establish an immediate first charge. A stated annual first charge does not transfer to the monthly plan.
- No free trial does not establish when billing, access, or the subscription begins.
- An optional consent choice does not establish unsubscribe mechanics, confirmation branching, merge fields, or future message frequency.
- Preserve the supplied consent scope exactly. Do not add content categories, cadence, frequency adjectives, or a delivery artifact such as a link or file.
- A required permission does not establish a manual fallback, skipped path, post-permission success, or extra data use.
- A supplied result is not a quotation, does not establish causality, and does not belong to a specific plan unless the brief says so.
- A capability such as SSO, regional hosting, migration support, or priority support does not establish implementation mechanics, service levels, staffing, response time, or an alternate support route.
- A named migration plan and migration manager may be stated as supplied, but do not assign the manager or plan any action the brief does not name. They do not establish written-plan delivery, end-to-end ownership, cutover responsibility, schedule authority, assignment timing, timeline confirmation, service coverage, or a reason migration duration varies.
- A requirement for internal review is not a requirement for approval and does not establish approval. Preserve the exact decision stage.
- The absence of on-premises deployment does not establish cloud-only delivery or exclude every other deployment model.
- A request form destination does not establish which fields it collects or what topics the later consultation covers.
- A supplied sensory note does not establish temporal progression, projection, persistence, noticeability, mood, or effect.

If a requested component cannot be completed without adding a fact, use accurate structure around the available facts. Do not invent the missing proposition and do not append a note about the omission unless the user requests analysis. Do not add edit rationale to a deliverable that asks only for copy or placement order.

### Benefit-first composition

Use a benefit-first opening as a strong candidate when a practical audience arrives with a defined job or problem. Keep emotion-first, identity-first, curiosity-first, mechanism-first, and proof-first openings available when the brief, audience, awareness, or category makes one of them stronger. Never make benefit-first structure universal.

### Decision-adjacent proof

Place the strongest relevant supplied proof near the uncertainty it resolves when the user asks for page composition. Trust signals belong near a consequential or risky action only when they answer the reader's live concern. Social proof must match the audience, use case, and decision. Do not scatter decorative badges, invent trust marks, or impose a fixed seal count.

For a payment-area composition, make the final commitment scannable: use a heading that signals purchase completion, explicitly remove unsupported badges, and relocate supplied verified proof into the payment area. When the brief supplies processor, charge, shipping, and return facts, use this decision order unless the user requires another: heading; processor line and verified proof; payment fields; shipping and return terms; `Your order will be charged immediately`; purchase button. The immediate-charge notice sits directly above the button, not earlier in combined supporting copy. Keep every ready-to-use line free of editorial directions such as `place the seal here`; put those directions after the composition.

Return the ready-to-use lines in that same order. The visible composition and the numbered placement order must describe one identical sequence; do not present combined supporting copy that puts the charge notice earlier and then claim that it sits above the button. Then provide the placement order. Put implementation changes such as `Remove the self-made badges`, `Move the verified seal from the footer`, and `Replace Continue with Complete purchase` after the ordered interface list, never as numbered interface elements.

### Field effort and qualification

When the user authorizes form or flow changes, ask only for inputs the current step genuinely needs. Preserve qualification fields, required permissions, and deliberate friction when they serve the stated business objective. Explain an extra field or permission at the point of decision when the brief supplies a truthful reason. Never claim that fewer fields always produce a better business result.

### Commitment staging

Match copy, detail, reassurance, and proof to the commitment being requested. A low-commitment download should not sound like an application. A purchase, payment, data connection, or annual contract should not hide terms, work, reversibility, or the next operational step supplied in the brief.

Name the immediate action and destination, not a later possibility. When submission starts review and a meeting may be offered afterward, the action requests review. Any mention of a meeting stays conditional. When submission sends a report by email, do not call the action an immediate download or invent a file type, link, merge field, delivery time, unsubscribe term, or confirmation branch.

For a report form whose only delivery field is work email, use neutral confirmation such as `Request received. We will send the report to your work email.` If optional marketing consent exists, keep it separate and unchecked, then add `You will receive marketing emails only if you selected the separate consent option.` Never claim the report is already sent, on its way, in the inbox, attached, or available through a link.

When an enterprise demo request starts specialist review, keep the familiar heading `Request an enterprise demo` and use the precise action `Send demo request for review`. State the form duration and no-payment term in short separate sentences. The confirmation starts with `Request received`, then states that review happens first. If specialist availability controls the next step, say that a meeting time may be offered only when the correct specialist is available, then state that submission does not guarantee a meeting. Never write `before offering a meeting time`, because it still promises the offer.

Strong confirmation pattern:

> Request received. A specialist will review your submission. If the correct specialist is available, you may be offered a meeting time. Submission does not guarantee a meeting.

Preserve every explicit funnel boundary the user supplies. If a download is not a sales application, a form submission does not book a meeting, or an action does not start a purchase, state that boundary in the requested composition instead of leaving it implicit.

When the only supplied downstream fact is that a paywall click does not start a subscription, use a continuation label such as `Continue with annual plan` rather than `Subscribe` or `Start subscription`. Adjacent microcopy must clarify both sides of the known boundary: `Continues to the next step. Your subscription does not start here.` This names the immediate transition without inventing checkout, review, payment, account, or confirmation behavior. Each complete variant must repeat every material price, charge, renewal, cancellation, access, and no-trial term needed to understand either choice. Keep full price units visible in every variant: a monthly price is `EUR [amount] per month`, not merely `EUR [amount]` under a monthly heading, and an annual price is `EUR [amount] per year`, not merely an amount beside annual-renewal copy. Do not invent a content or access scope such as `without limits`, `unlimited`, `full access`, or `all content`.

### Activation-aware onboarding

When the user supplies an activation event, write onboarding toward that event rather than permission acceptance, screen completion, or feature exposure alone. Use progressive disclosure. Explain required permissions where the decision occurs. Do not turn an analytics objective into a customer-facing claim. The name of an activation event establishes only that the event occurred. A successful account sync does not establish automatic budget construction, automatic updates, absence of manual entry, notification subjects, or any post-sync customer result. Under a closed fact set, do not invent a motivational before-state, problem contrast, data recency, estimate comparison, settings path, or reason the user should care. Neutral action framing is better than an unsupported benefit.

Treat activation and setup completion as separate states. A supplied first successful sync does not establish that setup, onboarding, account setup, or configuration is complete. Before that sync, orient to syncing the first account, not completing setup.

### Downstream evaluation

When the user requests an experiment plan, match the primary measure to the business decision and name guardrails that prevent a local win from hiding a downstream loss. Pricing and paywall tests may need revenue per visitor, retained revenue, refunds, or plan mix. Lead-generation tests may need qualified opportunities, cost per qualified lead, or revenue rather than raw completions. Use only metrics relevant to the supplied model and data.

Scale experiment-plan detail to the requested depth, but preserve the minimum controls needed to interpret the named business outcome. State stable assignment when repeat exposure is possible. Preserve every supplied denominator and time horizon, wait until that horizon is observable, distinguish the primary outcome from guardrails and diagnostics, and include a direction-neutral uncertainty decision. Concision compresses phrasing, not interpretability.

Write proposed test procedures as recommendations, not facts about the current analytics, checkout, accounting, refund window, payment method, customer behavior, cash flow, churn, or decision policy. Do not add a secondary business rationale merely because it could explain a result.

For an experiment with possible repeat exposure, use a stable assignment unit that prevents one participant from receiving both variants, and analyze participants by assigned variant. Specify the treatment using only available inputs rather than a vague instruction. Tie the decision to the supplied primary business outcome and supplied guardrails; do not substitute a click or intermediate action.

For a decision-grade experiment brief in an evidence review, include the controls required to run and interpret the test: a direction-neutral hypothesis; eligible population and necessary exclusions; stable assignment; unchanged control; one fully specified treatment; primary business outcome; downstream guardrails; diagnostic measures that do not determine adoption; preregistered sample-size, minimum-detectable-effect, duration, exclusion, and stopping rules; analysis by original assignment with uncertainty intervals; a measurement window long enough for supplied downstream outcomes; and symmetrical win, loss, null, and uncertain-result decisions. Match the eligible population to the primary-outcome denominator: a completed-first-order outcome requires new customers rather than a mixed new-and-returning population. These are proposed design controls, not claims about current instrumentation. Do not invent business-specific outcomes that the brief does not supply; label proposed guardrails as recommendations.

Treat sample-size, interval, and stopping methods as proposed procedures when the requested plan must support a reliable decision. Do not invent business metrics, financial adjustments, eligibility rules, instrumentation events, identifiers, thresholds, or decision policy. A named financial measure does not authorize adding chargebacks, fees, taxes, or another adjustment that the brief does not supply.

When reviewing a proposed rule for `every CTA`, distinguish commitment actions from navigation and informational controls. Test the disputed information at the final relevant commitment point unless the evidence supports a broader intervention. Keep the adoption rule internally consistent for wins, losses, null results, and uncertain results. Do not add an evidence grade, clarity claim, or fallback adoption rationale that the supplied evidence does not support.

## Build product-led and enterprise buying paths

### Separate routes by work required

When the brief supplies both self-serve and enterprise routes, make both routes legible without pretending they perform the same job. Give the self-serve buyer a direct action for trial, signup, or purchase. Give the enterprise buyer a separate request action for the supplied complexity, such as security review, procurement, migration, implementation, volume, or deployment planning. Prefer `Create [plan] account` for immediate account creation and `Request an enterprise consultation` for a consultation-request form; `Open the form` describes interface behavior but weakens the buyer's intended action. Use CTA labels and adjacent copy to make the division clear when readers need it. Do not promote a supplied migration manager into `managed migration`, or normalize a named deployment option into a broader deployment claim.

Do not treat the absence of a sales team as a conversion ideal or an eligibility test. Mature product-led businesses can use self-serve for adoption and sales for organizational complexity. When the product is genuinely sales-led, do not invent a signup, trial, public price, or instant-purchase route. When it is genuinely self-serve, do not force a sales conversation between the reader and the available action.

For a supplied enterprise route, make the CTA and adjacent microcopy state what submission does and does not do when the brief gives those boundaries. Generic enterprise capability does not authorize claims about spreadsheets replaced, integrations, migration sequence, rollout order, packaged scope, implementation-plan delivery, or post-review follow-up.

For a sales-led factory-planning route, the absence of self-serve, a trial, instant purchase, and public price does not establish the absence of a standard configuration, package, or deployment. Keep the composition to the supplied pre-quote plant-data review and implementation scoping, consultation form, required fields and assignment reason, review request, and no-meeting confirmation. Do not add a variability rationale, say requirements differ, claim that the scope becomes defined, invent a unifying product outcome, add a result of the review, or add a reason for reviewing an individual field.

### Make pricing a decision contract

Treat a pricing page as a complete buying decision, not a tariff. Use the applicable supplied information:

- who each plan is for;
- the visible entry price and billing period;
- usage, seat, capacity, or feature boundaries;
- trial length and card requirement;
- contract, renewal, cancellation, and implementation terms;
- what materially changes for enterprise;
- the action available for each route;
- proof that resolves a doubt at that decision.

Do not hide a supplied public price merely because a custom enterprise route also exists. Do not publish a private price, invent a discount, or add a public self-serve offer the user did not supply. When the brief intentionally withholds price, make the actual quoting or scoping process clear instead.

For a high-ticket self-serve plan, put destination microcopy immediately after each action. Show a combined amount under a timing label only when the brief explicitly gives that same timing for every included charge. Arithmetic can establish a total, not shared collection timing. Otherwise state each amount separately with only its supplied timing. State what the standard and custom actions open without claiming when a subscription, fee, or later process begins. Do not defer route consequences to a detached CTA-label list.

### Explain enterprise value through operational requirements

Lead with the outcome the enterprise buyer is trying to achieve. Then use only applicable supplied facts about scale, security, compliance, governance, administration, support, migration, onboarding, implementation, procurement, or professional services. Connect each fact to the uncertainty it resolves.

Do not use `enterprise-grade` as the sole explanation. Name what the product or service does differently for the larger organization. Do not invent certifications, service levels, deployment models, integrations, residency options, support promises, or implementation capacity.

Connect supplied capabilities to recognizable organizational requirements without assigning unsupplied product behavior. Prefer concrete capability sentences over abstract phrases such as `permission boundaries`, `implementation coordination`, or `defined support coverage` without explanation. Write `Employees can sign in through your organization's SAML setup` instead of `organizational sign-in route`. Role-based administration lets the organization assign administrative access by role, so different roles can carry different administrative access. EU or US region choice lets the organization choose whether its customer data is located in the EU or US. A security report available under NDA gives security and procurement teams a specific document to review. A named onboarding lead gives the organization a named contact; a migration plan gives stakeholders a plan for the move. Business-hours priority support tells buyers when priority support is available. Keep excluded offerings explicit. The exact availability line is `On-premises deployment is not available.` Never write `cloud-based`, `cloud deployment only`, or `cloud-only` unless that delivery model is supplied independently. Never upgrade a migration plan to a `documented plan`, `written plan`, `rollout plan`, or implementation process. The CTA is `Request an enterprise consultation` and points to the consultation-request form. Do not list form topics, collected requirements, or promised consultation coverage unless supplied. Keep internal calibration logic out of customer copy: do not explain what a claim does not imply unless the limitation itself belongs on the page.

Do not let enterprise claim safety flatten the argument into an evaluation checklist. Lead with the strongest supported organizational job or operational decision, then organize supplied capabilities as reasons tied to recognizable requirements. A reader job is not a product category, plan-selection instruction, evaluation process, CTA destination, or capability inventory. When no performance outcome is supplied, use the concrete task or decision enabled by the facts and do not invent a business result. Preserve exact capability names, limitations, route consequences, and qualification terms inside that argument. Put the primary action near the point where its purpose becomes clear; repeat it later only when the requested composition needs another decision point.

### Put commitment and proof beside the decision

Place trial duration, card requirement, billing period, annual commitment, renewal, cancellation, usage limits, and required implementation charges beside the action they qualify. Do not bury a material term in a distant FAQ or load every term into the button label.

Place proof where it resolves the live doubt. Scale proof can support capacity, a relevant customer case can support adoption, and a quantified result can support the result it actually measured. A large customer count does not prove implementation ease. A brand logo does not prove security. When no supplied proof answers the uncertainty, do not make decorative proof carry that claim.

For a multi-route composition, establish the strongest supported shared job before distinguishing buying routes. Avoid headlines that merely tell the reader to choose a plan, describe the buying process, or repeat the product category. Make each route's fit, material terms, action, and immediate destination legible without hiding the other route. Put general adoption proof where it supports the shared proposition. Put a platform-wide result after the route comparison, outside any one plan, with its attribution and causal limitation visible in the proof copy. Preserve an inclusion as an inclusion; do not rewrite it as a maximum.

When the brief supplies operational support but explicitly says the target performance is unmeasured, present the support and evidence gap as separate facts. The existence of a plan or named manager is not proof of downtime, duration, or success. Do not explain how the plan or manager works, what either controls, when either acts, or why outcomes vary unless the user supplies those facts.

For a migration proof section, do not rename a supplied migration manager as an owner, lead, contact, or coordinator. Do not claim the plan or manager prepares for, plans, reduces, controls, or changes cutover, downtime, duration, success, commitment, or approval. Keep the heading and body tied neutrally to the supplied internal cutover-review requirement, render the two supplied items below the body, use `Request migration planning`, and state the exact evidence gap.

Risk specificity can be contextual without becoming causal. When the buyer's only supplied requirement is a named concern, state that concern, place the supplied capability beside it, and state the evidence gap. Do not invent mechanics to create a stronger connection.

Do not turn a supplied result into a testimonial quote. Do not assign it to a pricing tier, product configuration, or customer segment that the brief did not name.

When a supplied customer is described as named but the name itself is absent, do not create a fill-in token and do not weaken the fact to merely `a customer`. Attribute it as `a named customer` and preserve every supplied causal limitation in adjacent visible copy. An implementation note explaining that proof does not imply causality does not satisfy this requirement.

Preserve proof form. Use quotation marks only for verbatim wording supplied by the user. A description of what a customer quote or statement says does not supply verbatim wording. When only its meaning is supplied, render a visible attributed paraphrase at the requested proof position without reconstructing speech. Do not return only an editorial placement instruction.

For a practical replacement-product composition, lead with the maintenance or replacement job and divide supplied operational facts across the opening instead of repeating one fact while omitting another. Keep compatibility, qualified intervals, ratings, installation cues, and pack quantity visible when supplied. Render requested customer proof as finished visible copy at its named position, then give the purchase action. The proof's visual position supplies placement, so do not add an editorial placement line inside finished page copy.

### Identity-led openings without invented provenance

Identity-led framing may create atmosphere from supplied sensory facts, but it may not invent when, why, or under what circumstances the product was created. A restrained sensory paragraph transforms supplied notes through texture, temperature, weight, dryness, softness, or contrast without asserting mood, attraction, longevity, projection, performance, or a creation story.

For a restrained nocturnal fragrance opening, make the headline express a reader identity or self-conception invited by the supplied nocturnal direction, not a coined product name, a note list, a product attribute such as `signature`, or a generic statement about night. When the brief gives aesthetic direction but no factual identity claim, use a reader callout such as `For those who keep their own hours`; it invites recognition without claiming that the product changes the wearer. Let the subhead carry product type, small-batch status, notes, and provenance. Give the sensory paragraph at least two concrete note relationships or contrasts. Use `Buy` for the purchase action unless the user's channel requires another verb.

Keep the factual detail line complete even when the subhead already carries one of those facts. Repetition inside the dedicated detail line is useful decision clarity, not decorative repetition.

Do not mistake the ban on invented effects for a ban on sensory writing. Ordinary material adjectives such as dry for cedar, tannic for black tea, and cool or powder-soft for iris describe the supplied notes; claims about mood, attraction, projection, longevity, noticeability, or performance describe unsupported effects. Write connected sensory sentences, not a punctuated repeat of the note list. Before returning, confirm that the sensory paragraph contains at least two concrete material adjectives and at least one relation or contrast among the notes.

### Preserve architecture, not competitor language

Use successful pages to study orientation, decision order, route separation, proof placement, and commitment disclosure. Do not copy a competitor's headline formula, current category fashion, or AI language merely because the company is successful. Current wording may postdate the growth, depend on brand familiarity, or serve a different audience.

## Calibrate research-derived guidance

For evidence-led or research-backed guidance, classify each conclusion internally before using it:

- `OBSERVED_COMMON`: present across the reviewed sample, with no causal claim;
- `CONTEXTUAL`: useful only under named audience, offer, stage, price, commitment, or delivery conditions;
- `HYPOTHESIS_TO_TEST`: plausible enough to compare, but not established for the user's business;
- `NOT_INFERABLE`: unsupported by the available measurement, such as conversion lift from public revenue, customer count, or page presence.

Keep these labels backstage unless the user asks for the research reasoning or decision record. Never turn cross-sectional page frequency into an expected effect size. Do not infer an optimal signup form from pages whose rendered fields were not observed.

## Resolve contradictions

When credible examples point in opposite directions, retain the moderator instead of choosing a universal winner. Compare the cases by audience, offer, funnel stage, commitment, price, traffic source, device, implementation, measurement window, and measured outcome.

When direction is unknown, state a non-directional hypothesis such as `may change`, not `will increase`. Match the proposed intervention to the scope of the rule being evaluated, or state and justify the narrower scope without pretending it tests the broader claim.

For an evidence-led recommendation, separate:

- observed result from proposed mechanism;
- intention from behavior;
- immediate action from downstream value;
- isolated wording from bundled UX changes;
- source context from the user's context.

Treat top-performing sites as composition references only. Performance rank does not identify which element caused the result. Treat vendor and practitioner cases as experiment seeds. Do not transport their effect sizes into another business.

Outside explicit review mode, preserve the user's supplied claims and framing. Only apply source grading, causal review, or effect-transfer limits when the user requests evidence review, research-backed guidance, or experiment interpretation.

## Evaluation contract

A conversion-context response fails when it replaces the stated business outcome with a nearby metric, converts a contextual prior into a universal law, transfers a reported effect size across contexts during requested review, invents proof or data-use reasons, changes required commitment or qualification without authorization, hides supplied prices, terms, or permissions, invents a route or enterprise capability, collapses materially different self-serve and enterprise actions into one ambiguous path, uses generic enterprise language instead of the supplied operational difference, separates proof from the uncertainty it resolves, or optimizes one element while weakening the full composition.

Evaluate outcome fit, funnel-stage fit, route fit, pricing-decision fit, enterprise-risk fit, proof-to-uncertainty fit, commitment fit, prior calibration, contradiction handling, first-read comprehension, concrete action clarity, brief fidelity, and composition fit.
